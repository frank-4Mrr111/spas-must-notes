Formula Sheet - SMS 3413

Accounting equation: Assets = Liabilities + Equity | Gross profit: Revenue - Cost of Sales | Profit: Gross Profit - Expenses + Other Income | Working capital: Current Assets - Current Liabilities | Current ratio: Current Assets / Current Liabilities | Quick ratio: (Current Assets - Inventory) / Current Liabilities | ROCE: Operating Profit / Capital Employed x 100% | ROE: Profit After Tax / Equity x 100% | Gross margin: Gross Profit / Revenue x 100% | Net margin: Profit After Tax / Revenue x 100% | Debt ratio: Total Liabilities / Total Assets | Cash flow: Operating + Investing + Financing = Change in Cash

Glossary - SMS 3413

Financial reporting: publishing what a company owns, owes, earns. IFRS: international reporting rules. Revenue: sales. Cost of sales: direct cost of goods sold. Asset: what company owns. Liability: what company owes. Equity: owners stake = Assets - Liabilities. Depreciation: spreading cost of asset over life. Accrual: record when earned / incurred, not when cash moves. Cash flow: cash in and out. Ratio: one number divided by another, to compare. Audit: independent check of accounts. Creative accounting: bending rules to look better.

Topic 1: Introduction to Financial Reporting - Users, Framework, Statements

Estimated time: ~30 minutes | Objectives: Define financial reporting, name users, name three statements, explain IFRS.

Financial reporting tells the truth about a company - in numbers, in a standard way, so anyone can compare.

Users: Investors, lenders, 【entity-IRA¦canonical_name=IRA】 / 【entity-RBA¦canonical_name=RBA】, tax (KRA), employees, suppliers, management.

Three statements: 1. Profit or Loss - did we make profit? 2. Financial Position - what do we own and owe? 3. Cash Flows - where did cash come from and go?

IFRS: International Financial Reporting Standards - rules all listed companies follow, so accounts are comparable. IFRS 17 is the special rule for insurance contracts.

Qualities: Relevant, faithful, comparable, understandable, timely.

Topic 2: Statement of Profit or Loss and Other Comprehensive Income

Estimated time: ~35 minutes | Objectives: Build profit or loss, calculate gross profit, operating profit, profit after tax.

Profit or Loss answers: How much did we sell, what did it cost, what is left?

Steps: Revenue - Cost of Sales = Gross Profit. Gross Profit - Expenses (salaries, rent, depreciation) + Other Income = Operating Profit. Operating Profit - Finance Cost - Tax = Profit After Tax.

Example: Revenue 10000000, Cost of Sales 6000000. Gross Profit = 4000000. Expenses 2000000. Operating Profit = 2000000. Finance 200000, Tax 360000. Profit After Tax = 1440000.

Other Comprehensive Income: Gains not in profit, e.g. revaluation of property. Total Comprehensive Income = Profit After Tax + OCI.

Topic 3: Statement of Financial Position - Assets, Liabilities, Equity

Estimated time: ~40 minutes | Objectives: Explain financial position, classify assets and liabilities, use accounting equation.

Financial Position is a photo at one date: What do we own? What do we owe? What is left for owners?

Equation: Assets = Liabilities + Equity. Always balances.

Assets: Non-current (building, motor vehicle, equipment - used >1 year), Current (inventory, receivables, cash - used <1 year).

Liabilities: Non-current (long loan), Current (payables, overdraft, tax due).

Equity: Share capital + Retained profits.

Example: Assets 50000000, Liabilities 30000000. Equity = 20000000.

Insurer special: Insurance liabilities - reserves for future claims - are the biggest liability.

Topic 4: Statement of Cash Flows

Estimated time: ~35 minutes | Objectives: Explain cash flows, distinguish operating, investing, financing, calculate change in cash.

Profit is not cash. A company can be profitable and still run out of cash. Cash Flow statement shows cash.

Operating: Cash from day-to-day business - customers pay, suppliers paid, salaries paid.

Investing: Cash buying / selling long assets - buy building, sell vehicle.

Financing: Cash from owners and lenders - issue shares, take loan, repay loan, pay dividend.

Example: Operating +5000000, Investing -3000000, Financing -1000000. Change in cash = +1000000. Opening cash 2000000. Closing cash = 3000000.

Topic 5: Ratio Analysis and Interpretation

Estimated time: ~40 minutes | Objectives: Explain ratios, calculate liquidity, profitability, gearing, interpret trends.

Ratios turn big numbers into meaning. One ratio alone means little - compare to last year, to competitors, to target.

Liquidity: Current ratio = Current Assets / Current Liabilities. Quick ratio = (Current Assets - Inventory) / Current Liabilities. Can we pay short debts?

Profitability: Gross margin = Gross Profit / Revenue. Net margin = Profit After Tax / Revenue. ROCE = Operating Profit / Capital Employed.

Gearing: Debt ratio = Total Liabilities / Total Assets. How much is borrowed?

Example: Current Assets 6000000, Current Liabilities 3000000. Current ratio = 2.0. Good - can pay twice over.

Topic 6: Financial Reporting for Insurers and Creative Accounting

Estimated time: ~30 minutes | Objectives: Explain insurer reporting, explain IFRS 17, explain creative accounting and how to spot it.

Insurers are special: they collect premium now, pay claims later. Their accounts must show if they can pay future claims.

Insurer statements: Premium earned, Claims incurred, Insurance reserves (liabilities), Investment income, Underwriting profit = Premium - Claims - Expenses.

IFRS 17: New rule - measure insurance contracts at present value of future cashflows + risk adjustment + contractual service margin. Makes insurers comparable worldwide.

Creative accounting: Bending rules - hiding debt, inflating profit, delaying expenses. Red flags: profit rises but cash falls, receivables rise faster than sales, frequent change of accounting policy.
