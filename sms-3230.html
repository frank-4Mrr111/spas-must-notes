Formula Sheet - SMS 3230

Expected Health Claims: Members x Utilisation Rate x Average Cost | Premium: Expected Claims + Expenses + Profit | IBNR: Claims incurred but not yet reported | Total Reserve: Outstanding + IBNR + Unexpired Risk | Loss Ratio: Claims / Premiums x 100% | Utilisation: Claims / Members

Glossary - SMS 3230

Health Insurance: pays for medical care. Morbidity: illness rate. Utilisation: how often people use care. Reserve: money set aside for future claims. IBNR: incurred but not reported. Outstanding Claims: reported but not yet paid. Unexpired Risk: cover still to run, premium already paid. Managed Care: insurer controls cost and quality via network. Capitation: fixed pay per member. Fee-for-service: pay for each service.

Topic 1: Introduction to Healthcare and Health Insurance

Estimated time: ~30 minutes | Objectives: Define healthcare, explain health insurance, explain why health is different from motor / fire.

Healthcare is care for illness and injury - hospital, doctor, medicine, surgery. Health insurance pays for that care, so one illness does not wipe out a family.

Why health is different: 1. Everyone needs it, often. 2. Costs are unpredictable and can be huge. 3. Demand is urgent - you cannot wait. 4. Information is unequal - doctor knows more than patient.

Who pays: Out-of-pocket, Employer, Insurer, Government / NHIF / SHA, Donor.

Topic 2: Types of Health Cover

Estimated time: ~30 minutes | Objectives: Explain inpatient, outpatient, maternity, dental, optical, group vs individual, exclusions.

Always answer: What it covers + Example + One exclusion.

Inpatient: Hospital admission, surgery, bed. Example: appendicitis operation.

Outpatient: Clinic visit, no admission. Example: malaria test + medicine.

Maternity: Pregnancy, delivery, antenatal. Often has waiting period.

Dental / Optical: Teeth, eyes. Often with limits.

Group vs Individual: Group = employer covers all staff, cheaper. Individual = you buy for yourself / family.

Topic 3: Health Costs, Morbidity and Utilisation

Estimated time: ~35 minutes | Objectives: Explain morbidity, utilisation, average cost, calculate expected claims.

Morbidity is how often people get ill. Utilisation is how often they use care. Average cost is how much each claim costs. Together they give expected claims.

Expected Claims = Members x Utilisation Rate x Average Cost.

Example: 1000 members, utilisation 0.8 claims per member per year, average cost 10000. Expected claims = 1000 x 0.8 x 10000 = 8000000.

What drives cost: Age, chronic illness, hospital prices, new technology, fraud, over-treatment.

Topic 4: Premiums for Health Insurance

Estimated time: ~35 minutes | Objectives: Explain health premium, calculate premium, explain loading, age, waiting period.

Health premium must cover expected claims + expenses + profit, with margin. Unlike life, health claims happen every year, so premium is reviewed every year.

Premium = Expected Claims + Expenses + Profit.

Example: Expected claims 8000000, expenses 1000000, profit 500000. Total = 9500000. Members 1000. Premium per member = 9500.

Rating factors: Age, family size, area, benefit level, claims history, waiting period.

Topic 5: Healthcare Reserves - IBNR, Outstanding and Unexpired

Estimated time: ~40 minutes | Objectives: Explain reserve, distinguish IBNR, outstanding, unexpired, calculate total reserve.

Reserve is money set aside for claims that have happened, or cover that has been paid for but not yet used. Without reserves, an insurer looks profitable today and fails tomorrow.

3 Reserves:

  1. Outstanding: Reported but not yet paid. Example: claim reported, hospital bill not yet settled.
  2. IBNR: Incurred But Not Reported. Example: patient treated in December, claim arrives in January.
  3. Unexpired Risk: Premium paid for cover still to run. Example: annual premium paid in January, 6 months still to run in July.

Total Reserve = Outstanding + IBNR + Unexpired.

Topic 6: Managed Care, Fraud and Regulation in Health

Estimated time: ~30 minutes | Objectives: Explain managed care, capitation vs fee-for-service, explain fraud, explain regulation.

Managed care tries to control cost and quality. The insurer does not just pay bills - it agrees prices, checks necessity, and uses a network of hospitals.

Capitation: Hospital gets fixed pay per member per month, whether they treat or not. Controls cost, but may under-treat.

Fee-for-service: Hospital gets paid for each service. May over-treat to earn more.

Fraud: Fake claims, inflated bills, unnecessary tests, sharing cards.

Regulation: IRA + Ministry of Health oversee insurers, hospitals, SHA / NHIF. Protect patients and policyholders.
