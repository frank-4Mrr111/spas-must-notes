Glossary — SMS 3214

lx: number alive at exact age x. dx: deaths between x and x+1. qx: probability a person aged x dies before x+1. px: probability they survive. ex: expected remaining years. Annuity: payments while alive. Assurance: payment on death. Premium: payment for cover. PV: present value.

Topic 1: Life Tables

Estimated time: ~30 minutes | Objectives: Read a life table, find lx, dx, qx, px, explain what each means.

A life table shows, for each age, how many people are alive, how many die, and chance of dying. It is foundation of all life insurance.

lx = number alive at exact age x. dx = deaths between x and x+1. dx = lx − lx+1

qx = probability a person aged x dies before x+1. qx = dxlx

px = probability they survive. px = 1 − qx

Worked Example: l30 = 95,000, l31 = 94,500. d30 = 95,000 − 94,500 = 500. q30 = 50095000 = 0.005263. p30 = 1 − 0.005263 = 0.994737.

Common exam mistake: Using dx as qx. dx is number of people (500). qx is probability (0.005263). Always divide.

Topic 2: Survival and Death Probabilities

Estimated time: ~30 minutes | Objectives: Find tpx and tqx, explain in words.

tpx = probability a person aged x survives t more years. tqx = probability they die within t years. They add to 1.

tpx = lx+tlx | tqx = 1 − tpx

Worked Example: l30=95,000, l35=93,000. 5p30 = 9300095000 = 0.9789. 5q30 = 0.0211.

Common exam mistake: Forgetting to subtract from 1 for q. Bottom is always lx.

Topic 3: Life Expectancy

Estimated time: ~25 minutes | Objectives: Explain ex, find it, interpret it.

Life expectancy ex = expected remaining years for a person aged x. It is an average, not a promise.

ex = Txlx

Worked Example: T60 = 1,200,000, l60 = 80,000. e60 = 120000080000 = 15 years. So a 60-year-old is expected to live to 75 on average.

Common exam mistake: Saying "will live 15 more years". No — on average.

Topic 4: Life Annuities

Estimated time: ~35 minutes | Objectives: Explain annuity, distinguish äx vs ax, find PV.

An annuity is a series of payments while you are alive. A pension is an annuity.

äx = annuity-due: first payment NOW. ax = annuity-immediate: first payment in 1 year. ax = äx − 1.

Worked Example: If ä65 = 12.5, then a65 = 11.5. If you pay 10,000 per year, PV = 10,000 × 11.5 = 115,000 for immediate annuity.

Common exam mistake: Mixing ä and a. "First payment now" = ä. "First payment in one year" = a.

Topic 5: Life Assurances

Estimated time: ~35 minutes | Objectives: Explain assurance, distinguish whole life vs term, find PV.

Life assurance pays on death. Term pays only if you die within term. Whole life pays whenever you die.

Ax = PV of 1 paid on death of person aged x.

Worked Example: If A40 = 0.25, PV of 100,000 whole life cover = 100,000 × 0.25 = 25,000.

Common exam mistake: Saying assurance pays while alive. No — assurance pays on death. Annuity pays while alive.

Topic 6: Premiums

Estimated time: ~35 minutes | Objectives: Explain net premium, find it, explain gross vs net.

Premium is what you pay for cover. Net premium covers benefits only — no expenses, no profit. Gross = net + expenses + profit.

Net Annual Premium P = PV of BenefitsPV of Annuity of 1 per year

Example: PV Benefits = 25,000, äx = 12.5. P = 2500012.5 = 2,000 per year.

Worked Example: PV Benefits = 30,000, ä40 = 15. P = 30,000 ÷ 15 = 2,000 per year.

Common exam mistake: Dividing by wrong annuity — use ä if premiums paid at start of year, a if at end.
