What Is This Unit About?

Actuarial Mathematics I is about how long people live, and how much money that means for insurance and pensions. If an insurance company does not know how long people live, it cannot know how much to charge. This unit teaches you the maths behind that.

You do not need to have attended a lecture. We start from zero.

Topic 1: Life Tables - Start From Zero

a) What is it?

A life table tells you: out of 100,000 babies born, how many are still alive at each age.

b) Why does it matter?

In Kenya, NHIF / an insurance company uses a life table to decide how much premium a 30-year-old should pay.

c) Formula - every symbol named

qx =dxlxandpx = 1 - qx

lx = number alive at age x | dx = deaths between age x and x+1 | qx = probability of dying at age x | px = probability of surviving age x

d) Worked Example - MUST style, every step

Question: l30 = 90,000 and d30 = 900. Find q30 and p30.

Step 1: qx = dxlx

Step 2: q30 = 90090,000

Step 3: = 0.01   Step 4: p30 = 1 - 0.01 = 0.99

Answer: q30 = 0.01, p30 = 0.99

e) Try It Yourself

Common mistake: swapping q and p, or swapping l and d. Remember: d is deaths (small), l is alive (big). q = small / big.

Topic 3: Life Expectancy - Start From Zero

What: How many more years a person aged x is expected to live. Why: Pensions need it — how long will we pay?

ex =Txlx

Tx = total years lived by all people aged x and older | lx = alive at age x

Worked Example: l60 = 80,000, T60 = 1,600,000. e60 = 1,600,00080,000 = 20 years.

Topic 4: Life Annuities - Start From Zero

What: A series of payments while you are alive. Why: Pension = annuity. You get paid every year you survive.

Present Value = Annual Payment × Annuity Factor

PV = Payment × äx   |   äx = value today of 1 paid every year while alive

Worked Example: Payment 10,000 per year, ä60 = 12. PV = 10,000 × 12 = 120,000.

Topic 5: Life Assurances - Start From Zero

What: A lump sum paid when you die. Why: Family gets money if you die. Opposite of annuity — annuity pays if you live, assurance pays if you die.

Present Value = Sum Assured × Assurance Factor

PV = Sum Assured × Ax

Worked Example: Sum Assured 100,000, A = 0.35. PV = 100,000 × 0.35 = 35,000.

Topic 6: Premiums - Start From Zero

What: The price you pay for insurance. Why: Premium must equal value of benefit. If benefit is worth 35,000 today, and you pay over years while alive, premium = benefit / annuity.

Annual Premium P =Value of Benefit (A)Annuity Factor (ä)

Worked Example: Benefit value 35,000, ä = 14. P = 35,00014 = 2,500 per year.
