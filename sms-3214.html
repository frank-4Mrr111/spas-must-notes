Glossary — SMS 3214

lx: number alive at exact age x. dx: deaths between x and x+1. qx: probability a person aged x dies before x+1. px: probability they survive. ex: expected remaining years. Annuity: payments while alive. Assurance: payment on death. Premium: payment for the cover. PV: present value — today's value of future money.

Topic 1: Life Tables

Estimated time: ~30 minutes  |  Objectives: Read a life table, find lx, dx, qx, px, and explain what each means.

A life table is a table that shows, for each age, how many people are alive, how many die, and the chance of dying. It is the foundation of all life insurance. Without it, you cannot price anything.

lx = number alive at exact age x. Example: l30 = 95,000 means 95,000 people reach age 30.

dx = deaths between age x and x+1. Formula: dx = lx − lx+1

qx = probability a person aged x dies before x+1. Formula: qx = dxlx

px = probability they survive. Formula: px = 1 − qx

Worked Example: l30 = 95,000, l31 = 94,500. Step 1: d30 = 95,000 − 94,500 = 500. Step 2: q30 = 50095000 = 0.005263. Step 3: p30 = 1 − 0.005263 = 0.994737.

Common exam mistake: Using dx as qx. dx is a number of people (500). qx is a probability (0.005263). Always divide.

Topic 2: Survival and Death Probabilities

Estimated time: ~30 minutes  |  Objectives: Find tpx and tqx, and explain them in words.

tpx = probability a person aged x survives t more years. tqx = probability they die within t years. They add to 1.

tpx = lx+tlx  |  tqx = 1 − tpx

Example in words: 5p30 = chance a 30-year-old lives to 35.

Worked Example: l30=95,000, l35=93,000. 5p30 = 9300095000 = 0.9789. 5q30 = 1 − 0.9789 = 0.0211.

Common exam mistake: Forgetting to subtract from 1 for q. And using lx+t on bottom — bottom is always lx.

Topic 3: Life Expectancy

Estimated time: ~25 minutes  |  Objectives: Explain ex, find it from Tx and lx, and interpret it.

Life expectancy ex = expected remaining years for a person aged x. It is an average, not a promise. Some live longer, some shorter.

ex = Txlx   where Tx = total years lived from age x onwards.

Worked Example: T60 = 1,200,000, l60 = 80,000. e60 = 120000080000 = 15 years. So a 60-year-old is expected to live to 75 on average.

Common exam mistake: Saying "a 60-year-old will live 15 more years". No — on average. Always add "on average".

Topic 4: Life Annuities

Estimated time: ~35 minutes  |  Objectives: Explain annuity, distinguish äx vs ax, find PV of annuity.

An annuity is a series of payments while you are alive. A pension is an annuity. You pay while working, you receive while retired — but only if you survive to each payment.

äx = annuity-due: first payment NOW, at age x. ax = annuity-immediate: first payment in 1 year. So ax = äx − 1.

PV of annuity = sum of (payment × survival probability × discount factor). You do not get paid if you die, so each payment is weighted by chance you are alive.

Worked Example: If ä65 = 12.5, then a65 = 12.5 − 1 = 11.5. If you pay 10,000 per year, PV = 10,000 × 11.5 = 115,000 for immediate annuity.

Common exam mistake: Mixing ä and a. If question says "first payment now" use ä. If "first payment in one year" use a.

Topic 5: Life Assurances

Estimated time: ~35 minutes  |  Objectives: Explain assurance, distinguish whole life vs term, find PV of assurance.

Life assurance pays on death. Term assurance pays only if you die within the term. Whole life assurance pays whenever you die.

Ax = PV of 1 paid on death of a person aged x. Whole life: Ax. Term n years: Ax:n1.

PV = sum of (payment × probability of dying in that year × discount factor).

Worked Example: If A40 = 0.25, PV of 100,000 whole life cover = 100,000 × 0.25 = 25,000. That is the single premium for the cover, before expenses and profit.

Common exam mistake: Saying assurance pays while alive. No — assurance pays on death. Annuity pays while alive. They are opposites.

Topic 6: Premiums

Estimated time: ~35 minutes  |  Objectives: Explain net premium, find it from PV benefits / PV annuity, and explain gross vs net.

Premium is what you pay for cover. Net premium covers benefits only — no expenses, no profit. Gross premium = net premium + expenses + profit margin.

Net Annual Premium P = PV of BenefitsPV of Annuity of 1 per year

Example: PV Benefits = 25,000, äx = 12.5. P = 2500012.5 = 2,000 per year.

Worked Example: PV Benefits = 30,000, ä40 = 15. P = 30,000 ÷ 15 = 2,000 per year. If you pay for life, you pay 2,000 each year while alive.

Common exam mistake: Dividing by the wrong annuity — use ä if premiums are paid at start of year (due), a if at end. Read the question.
