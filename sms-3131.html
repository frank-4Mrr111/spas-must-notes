Glossary - SMS 3113

Actuary: professional who uses maths, statistics and finance to measure risk. Risk: chance of loss. Uncertainty: not knowing what will happen. Insurance: pooling risk, insured pays premium, insurer pays claims. Premium: price of insurance. Policy: contract. Claim: request for payment. Pooling: many people contribute, few claim. Interest: price of money over time. Mortality: death rate. Reserve: money set aside for future claims.

Topic 1: What is Actuarial Science?

Estimated time: ~30 minutes | Objectives: Define actuarial science, explain what an actuary does, distinguish actuary from accountant / statistician, explain why actuaries are needed.

Actuarial science is the study of risk using maths, statistics and finance. An actuary measures how likely a loss is, how big it will be, and how much money must be set aside today to pay it tomorrow.

What an actuary does:

  1. Measures risk - how likely is death, illness, accident, retirement?
  2. Prices it - what premium should be charged?
  3. Reserves for it - how much to keep for future claims?
  4. Advises - is the company safe? Should it change prices?

Actuary vs Accountant vs Statistician: Accountant records what already happened. Statistician finds patterns in data. Actuary uses both to predict future money needs and price risk.

Common exam mistake: Saying 'actuary does maths' and stopping. Always add risk + money + future.

Topic 2: The Actuarial Profession and Ethics

Estimated time: ~30 minutes | Objectives: Explain professionalism, name actuarial bodies, explain ethics, explain why trust matters.

An actuary is a professional, like a doctor or lawyer. You must be honest, competent, and put the client and public first, even when it costs you money.

Professional duties:

  1. Competence - only do work you are trained for.
  2. Honesty - do not hide bad news, do not invent numbers.
  3. Confidentiality - do not share client data.
  4. Public interest - do not help a company cheat policyholders.

Bodies: In Kenya, actuaries work under the Actuarial Society of Kenya (TASK) and follow international standards. Globally, the Institute and Faculty of Actuaries (IFoA) and Society of Actuaries (SOA) set exams and conduct rules.

Why trust matters: People pay premiums today for a promise in 20 years. If they do not trust actuaries, the whole system collapses.

Topic 3: Insurance Principles and Types

Estimated time: ~35 minutes | Objectives: Explain insurance, explain 6 principles, distinguish life vs general insurance, give examples.

Insurance is pooling: many people pay a small premium, the few who suffer loss are paid from the pool. No one person could pay a 1,000,000 claim alone. Together, they can.

6 Principles:

  1. Insurable interest - you must lose if the thing is lost.
  2. Utmost good faith - tell the truth, hide nothing important.
  3. Indemnity - you are put back, not made richer (general insurance).
  4. Subrogation - insurer can recover from the person who caused loss.
  5. Contribution - if 2 insurers cover same loss, they share.
  6. Proximate cause - the main cause must be covered.

Types: Life insurance - pays on death / survival / retirement. General insurance - motor, fire, health, liability. Health / Pension - care and retirement income.

Topic 4: Risk, Uncertainty and Pooling

Estimated time: ~30 minutes | Objectives: Define risk, distinguish risk vs uncertainty, explain pooling and law of large numbers, explain types of risk.

Risk is the chance of loss. Uncertainty is not knowing what will happen. You can have uncertainty without risk - e.g. not knowing if it will rain. Risk needs loss - e.g. rain destroys crops.

Pooling + Law of Large Numbers: With 10 people, claims are wild. With 100,000 people, the average claim becomes stable and predictable. That is why insurers need many policyholders.

Types of risk: Pure risk (only loss - fire), Speculative risk (loss or gain - gambling / business), Fundamental risk (affects many - flood), Particular risk (affects one - theft).

Risk management: Avoid it, Reduce it, Transfer it (insurance), Retain it (pay yourself).

Topic 5: Interest, Mortality and Basic Actuarial Calculations

Estimated time: ~35 minutes | Objectives: Explain simple vs compound interest, calculate amount, explain mortality rate, calculate expected claims.

Money has time value: 1000 today is worth more than 1000 in a year, because you can invest it. Interest is the price of waiting.

Simple Interest: I = P x r x t. Example: P=10000, r=10% = 0.10, t=2 years. I = 10000 x 0.10 x 2 = 2000. Amount = 12000.

Compound Interest: A = P(1+i)^n. Example: P=10000, i=10%, n=2. A = 10000 x 1.10^2 = 10000 x 1.21 = 12100.

Mortality: q = Deaths / Exposed. If 5 die out of 1000, q = 5/1000 = 0.005.

Expected claims: If 1000 policies, q=0.005, average claim 200000: Expected deaths = 1000 x 0.005 = 5. Expected claims = 5 x 200000 = 1,000,000.

Topic 6: Where Actuaries Work and What They Do

Estimated time: ~30 minutes | Objectives: List where actuaries work, explain daily work, explain skills needed, explain career path.

Actuaries do not only work in insurance. They work in pensions, health, banking, consulting, government and regulation.

Where: Life insurer (pricing, reserves), General insurer, Pension fund, Health insurer, Consulting firm, Regulator / Central Bank, Bank / Investment.

Daily work: Collect data, check it, build a model, calculate premiums / reserves, write a report, explain to managers who are not actuaries.

Skills: Maths + Statistics, Excel / R / Python, Communication, Ethics, Business understanding.

Career: Degree -> actuarial exams (IFoA / SOA / TASK) -> student -> Associate -> Fellow. Exams take years. That is normal.
