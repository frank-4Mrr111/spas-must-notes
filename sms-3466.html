Glossary - SMS 3466

Survival time: time until event happens. Event: death, claim, lapse, failure. Censoring: event not seen by end of study - we only know survival > time. Truncation: person only enters study if they survive to a point. Survival function S(t): chance of surviving past t. Hazard h(t): instant risk of event at t, given survival to t. Cumulative hazard H(t): total hazard up to t. Kaplan-Meier: non-parametric estimate of S(t). Nelson-Aalen: estimate of H(t). Parametric: assume a shape (Exponential, Weibull). Cox model: hazard depends on covariates, baseline hazard not assumed. Hazard ratio: how many times higher is risk for one group vs another. Life table: q_x, l_x, d_x by age.

Topic 1: Introduction to Survival Data - Time to Event, Censoring, Truncation

Estimated time: ~30 minutes | Objectives: Define survival time, explain censoring and truncation, give actuarial examples.

Survival analysis = time until something happens. For an actuary: time until death, time until a policy lapses, time until a claim.

Survival time T: Time from start to event. Example: Person joins at age 40, dies at 75. T = 35 years.

Censoring: Study ends before event. Example: Study runs 5 years, person still alive at end. We know T > 5, not exact T. Right-censoring is most common.

Truncation: Person only enters if they survive to entry. Example: Study only includes people alive at age 60. Those who died before 60 are never seen.

Why special methods: You cannot just drop censored people - that biases results. Kaplan-Meier uses them until they leave.

Topic 2: Survival, Hazard and Cumulative Hazard Functions

Estimated time: ~40 minutes | Objectives: Define S(t), h(t), H(t), convert between them, calculate S(t) from hazard.

Three functions describe the same thing. Learn to move between them.

Survival S(t) = P(T > t): Chance of surviving past t. S(0)=1, S falls to 0.

Hazard h(t): Instant risk at t, given you survived to t. Like force of mortality. High hazard = high instant risk.

Cumulative hazard H(t) = -ln S(t): Total hazard up to t. So S(t) = exp(-H(t)).

Example: Constant hazard h=0.1 per year. H(10)=0.1x10=1. S(10)=exp(-1)=0.368. So 36.8% survive 10 years.

Density: f(t) = h(t) x S(t).

Topic 3: Kaplan-Meier and Nelson-Aalen Estimators

Estimated time: ~40 minutes | Objectives: Explain Kaplan-Meier, calculate S_hat, explain Nelson-Aalen.

Kaplan-Meier (KM) estimates S(t) from real data, with censoring. No shape assumed.

KM: At each event time, multiply by (1 - d / n). d = deaths at that time, n = at risk just before. Censored people drop from n after that time, but do not count as deaths.

Example: Start n=20. At t1, d=4. S = 1 - 4/20 = 0.8. Now n=16. At t2, d=4. S = 0.8 x (1 - 4/16) = 0.8 x 0.75 = 0.6.

Nelson-Aalen: H_hat = sum (d / n). Same data: H = 4/20 + 4/16 = 0.2 + 0.25 = 0.45. Then S = exp(-0.45) = 0.638, close to KM 0.6.

Topic 4: Parametric Models - Exponential, Weibull, Lognormal

Estimated time: ~35 minutes | Objectives: Explain parametric models, calculate S(t) for Exponential and Weibull, compare shapes.

Parametric = assume a shape. Easier to extrapolate, but wrong shape = wrong answers.

Exponential: Constant hazard lambda. S(t) = exp(-lambda t). Mean = 1/lambda. Median = ln 2 / lambda. Example: lambda=0.02. Mean = 50 years. S(50) = exp(-1) = 0.368.

Weibull: Hazard rises or falls. S(t) = exp(-(t/theta)^alpha). alpha > 1: hazard rises with age (old age). alpha < 1: hazard falls. Example: theta=100, alpha=2, t=50. S = exp(-(0.5)^2) = exp(-0.25) = 0.779.

Lognormal: Hazard rises then falls. Good for some repair / lapse times.

Topic 5: Cox Proportional Hazards and Log-Rank Test

Estimated time: ~40 minutes | Objectives: Explain Cox model, interpret hazard ratio, explain log-rank test.

Cox model asks: Does smoking, sex, or age raise hazard? Without assuming a shape for baseline hazard.

Cox: h(t | x) = h_0(t) x exp(beta x). h_0 = baseline hazard (when x=0). exp(beta x) scales it up or down.

Hazard ratio HR = exp(beta): HR=2 means group has double hazard at every time. HR=1 means no effect. HR=0.5 means half hazard.

Example: beta=0.693 for smoker vs non-smoker. HR = exp(0.693) = 2. Smokers have double hazard.

Log-rank test: Tests if two KM curves differ. Like chi-square. p < 0.05 = curves differ significantly.

Proportional hazards: HR stays same over time. Check: KM curves should not cross.

Topic 6: Survival Analysis in Actuarial Practice - Life Tables, Kenya

Estimated time: ~30 minutes | Objectives: Apply survival to life tables, pensions, lapses, explain Kenya practice.

Life tables are survival analysis in a table. Actuaries use them to price life insurance and pensions.

Life table: l_x = alive at age x, d_x = deaths between x and x+1, q_x = d_x / l_x = chance of dying in year. p_x = 1 - q_x.

Example: l_60=10000, d_60=200. q_60 = 200/10000 = 0.02. p_60 = 0.98.

Uses: 1. Price life insurance - chance of death. 2. Price annuities / pensions - chance of survival. 3. Lapse - chance policy ends. 4. Claim - time to claim.

Kenya: Need Kenya life tables - mortality differs from UK / US tables. HIV, malaria, accidents matter. Data: short, incomplete, under-reporting. Use graduation - smooth rough rates.
