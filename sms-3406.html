Glossary - SMS 3406

Survival time: time until event. Event: death, failure, recovery. Censoring: event not seen by end of study. Right censoring: still alive at end. Truncation: only some people enter study. Survival function: probability of surviving past t. Hazard: instant risk at t, given survived to t. Kaplan-Meier: non-parametric survival estimate. Life table: grouped survival estimate. Cox model: hazard depends on covariates. Proportional hazards: hazard ratio stays constant.

Topic 1: Introduction to Survival Data - Censoring and Truncation

Estimated time: ~30 minutes | Objectives: Define survival time, explain censoring, distinguish right, left, interval censoring and truncation.

Survival data measures time until an event. The problem: we do not always see the event.

Censoring: Event not observed. Right censoring: patient still alive at end of study, or lost to follow-up. Left censoring: event happened before study started. Interval censoring: event happened between two visits, we only know the interval.

Truncation: Person never enters study unless they survive to a date. Example: only people alive at age 60 are studied.

Why it matters: If you ignore censoring and treat censored times as deaths, you underestimate survival. If you drop them, you waste data and bias results.

Topic 2: Survival, Hazard and Cumulative Hazard Functions

Estimated time: ~35 minutes | Objectives: Define S(t), f(t), h(t), H(t), derive relationships, calculate values.

Four functions describe the same story.

Survival: S(t) = P(T > t). Starts at 1, falls to 0.

Density: f(t) = -S'(t).

Hazard: h(t) = f(t) / S(t). Instant risk at t, given survived to t.

Cumulative hazard: H(t) = -ln S(t). So S(t) = exp(-H(t)).

Example: S(5) = 0.8. H(5) = -ln 0.8 = 0.2231. If h is constant 0.1, S(10) = exp(-0.1 x 10) = exp(-1) = 0.3679.

Topic 3: Kaplan-Meier and Nelson-Aalen Estimators

Estimated time: ~40 minutes | Objectives: Explain KM, calculate KM survival, explain Nelson-Aalen, interpret steps.

Kaplan-Meier is the workhorse. It updates survival only at event times, and uses only those still at risk.

KM: S-hat(t) = Product over event times ≤ t of (1 - d_i / n_i), where d_i = deaths at time i, n_i = at risk just before i.

Example: At time 2: n=10, d=2. Factor = 1 - 2/10 = 0.8. S-hat(2) = 0.8. At time 5: n=7 (one censored), d=1. Factor = 1 - 1/7 = 0.8571. S-hat(5) = 0.8 x 0.8571 = 0.6857.

Nelson-Aalen: H-hat(t) = Sum d_i / n_i. Then S-hat = exp(-H-hat).

Topic 4: Life Tables and Actuarial Estimator

Estimated time: ~35 minutes | Objectives: Explain life table, calculate actuarial estimator, explain grouping.

Life tables group time into intervals - years, months. The actuarial estimator assumes censoring happens uniformly in the interval.

Actuarial: Effective at risk n' = n - c/2, where c = censored in interval. q = d / n'. S = Product (1 - q).

Example: Interval 0-1 year: n=100, d=10, c=20. n' = 100 - 10 = 90. q = 10/90 = 0.1111. S(1) = 0.8889.

KM vs Life table: KM uses exact times, life table groups. KM is better with small data, life table is clearer for reports and old actuarial work.

Topic 5: Parametric Models - Exponential, Weibull, Lognormal

Estimated time: ~35 minutes | Objectives: Explain parametric models, fit exponential, distinguish Weibull shape, interpret parameters.

Parametric models assume a shape for survival. They use all data and can predict beyond the study.

Exponential: h(t) = lambda constant. S(t) = exp(-lambda t). Mean = 1/lambda. Memoryless - old and young have same risk. Good for random failures, bad for human mortality.

Weibull: h(t) = lambda p (lambda t)^(p-1). If p > 1, hazard rises with age. If p < 1, hazard falls. If p = 1, it is exponential.

Lognormal: log T is normal. Hazard rises then falls. Good for some diseases.

Example: Exponential lambda = 0.1. S(10) = exp(-1) = 0.3679. Median = ln 2 / lambda = 6.93.

Topic 6: Cox Proportional Hazards and Comparing Groups

Estimated time: ~35 minutes | Objectives: Explain Cox model, interpret hazard ratio, explain log-rank test, compare groups.

Cox model lets hazard depend on covariates - age, sex, treatment - without assuming a shape for baseline hazard.

Cox: h(t|x) = h0(t) x exp(beta x). h0 is baseline hazard. exp(beta) is hazard ratio.

Interpretation: Hazard ratio 2.0 = twice the risk at any time. Hazard ratio 0.5 = half the risk. Hazard ratio 1.0 = no difference.

Example: Treatment beta = -0.693. Hazard ratio = exp(-0.693) = 0.5. Treated patients have half the hazard of untreated.

Log-rank test: Tests if two survival curves differ, without assuming a model. p < 0.05 = curves differ significantly.
