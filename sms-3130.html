Glossary - SMS 3130

Actuarial Control Cycle: loop of specify, solve, monitor, review. Assumption: best estimate of future, e.g. mortality, interest. Experience: what actually happened. Model: simplified maths of reality. Professionalism: competence + honesty + public interest. Conflict of interest: your interest clashes with client interest. Whistleblowing: reporting wrongdoing. Governance: who decides, who checks.

Topic 1: Actuarial Practice and the Control Cycle

Estimated time: ~30 minutes | Objectives: Explain actuarial practice, explain control cycle, apply it to a case.

Actuarial practice is not one calculation. It is a loop you repeat: you specify the problem, you build a solution, you watch what happens, you fix it.

Control Cycle:

  1. Specify - What is the problem? E.g. price a new health product.
  2. Develop - Choose assumptions, build model, calculate premium.
  3. Monitor - Compare actual claims vs expected. Is it working?
  4. Review / Revise - Change price, assumptions, or product.

Why a cycle: The world changes. Mortality falls, interest falls, claims rise. A one-time price will be wrong. You must loop.

Topic 2: Professionalism and Code of Conduct

Estimated time: ~30 minutes | Objectives: Explain professionalism, explain code of conduct, explain competence and confidentiality.

Professionalism means: you can be trusted even when no one is watching. The code of conduct is the written rules for that trust.

Code covers: Competence - only do work you are trained for. Integrity - do not lie, do not hide. Confidentiality - do not share client data. Objectivity - do not let bias change numbers. Public interest - protect policyholders, not just your boss.

Discipline: Break the code and you can be warned, suspended, or expelled. That is why it works.

Topic 3: Ethics, Conflicts and Whistleblowing

Estimated time: ~35 minutes | Objectives: Explain ethics, explain conflict of interest, explain whistleblowing, judge cases.

Ethics is choosing right when it costs you. A conflict of interest is when your own gain, or your boss's gain, pulls against your duty to the client.

Examples: Boss wants lower reserves to show profit. Client wants you to hide a risk. You own shares in the company you are valuing.

What to do: Disclose the conflict, refuse the wrong instruction, document it, escalate, and if needed whistleblow - report to regulator / professional body.

Whistleblowing is protected: You must not be punished for reporting real wrongdoing in good faith.

Topic 4: Assumptions, Models and Experience

Estimated time: ~35 minutes | Objectives: Explain assumptions, explain models, explain experience monitoring, explain what to do when experience differs.

An assumption is your best guess about the future: mortality, interest, expenses, lapses. A model is maths that turns assumptions into a premium or reserve. Experience is what actually happened.

Monitoring: Expected claims 10000000, Actual claims 12000000. Difference = 2000000 worse. You must ask: Was it random? Was the assumption wrong? Was data wrong? Then revise.

Good assumption: Based on data, realistic, documented, reviewed. Bad assumption: copied, hidden, never checked.

Topic 5: Communication and Reporting

Estimated time: ~30 minutes | Objectives: Explain why communication matters, explain good report, explain communicating to non-actuaries.

The best calculation is useless if no one understands it. Actuaries must explain to managers, boards, regulators and policyholders who are not actuaries.

Good report: Clear purpose, Key result first, Assumptions stated, Uncertainty stated, Recommendation, What to do next.

Bad report: 50 pages of formulas, no conclusion, no uncertainty, jargon.

Rule: If a manager cannot repeat your conclusion after 2 minutes, you failed to communicate.

Topic 6: Regulation, Governance and Future

Estimated time: ~30 minutes | Objectives: Explain regulation, explain governance, explain future challenges.

Regulation protects policyholders. Governance decides who checks the actuary. The future brings climate, AI, pandemics, and new products - actuaries must adapt.

Regulator (IRA in Kenya): Licenses insurers, checks solvency, requires actuarial reports, protects policyholders.

Governance: Board approves, Actuary calculates, Auditor checks, Regulator oversees. No one person controls everything.

Future: Climate risk, Cyber risk, AI models, Longer life, Micro-insurance for low-income customers.
