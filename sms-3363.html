Glossary - SMS 3363

Risk: chance of loss. Claim frequency: how many claims. Claim severity: how big each claim is. Aggregate claims: total claims in a period. Premium: price of insurance. Loading: extra for expenses, profit, safety. Reinsurance: insurance for insurers. Retention: what insurer keeps. Ruin: insurer runs out of money. Credibility: how much to trust own data. Excess: amount insurer pays before reinsurer pays.

Topic 1: Risk, Loss Distributions and Claim Numbers

Estimated time: ~35 minutes | Objectives: Explain risk, explain frequency, use Poisson, calculate probabilities.

Risk theory starts with two questions: How many claims? How big?

Frequency (N): Number of claims. Often Poisson: P(N=n) = e^-lambda x lambda^n / n!, E[N] = lambda, Var[N] = lambda.

Example: lambda = 2 claims per year. P(N=0) = e^-2 = 0.1353. P(N=1) = e^-2 x 2 = 0.2707. P(N=2) = e^-2 x 4 / 2 = 0.2707.

Why Poisson? Claims are rare, independent, and happen one at a time - like accidents in a town.

Topic 2: Claim Severity - Individual Claims

Estimated time: ~35 minutes | Objectives: Explain severity, calculate mean and variance, explain heavy tail.

Severity (X) is size of one claim. A motor claim may be 50000, a fire claim may be 5000000.

Mean severity: E[X] = average claim size. Second moment: E[X^2] - needed for variance of total claims.

Example: Claims 10000, 20000, 30000. E[X] = 20000. E[X^2] = (10000^2 + 20000^2 + 30000^2)/3 = (100M + 400M + 900M)/3 = 466.67M.

Heavy tail: A few huge claims dominate total. Fire and liability have heavy tails - most claims small, one claim huge.

Topic 3: Aggregate Claims - Compound Distributions

Estimated time: ~40 minutes | Objectives: Explain aggregate claims S, calculate E[S] and Var[S], explain compound Poisson.

Aggregate claims S = X1 + X2 + ... + XN. Total paid in a year. This is what the insurer must actually pay.

Compound Poisson: N ~ Poisson(lambda), X = severity. Then E[S] = E[N] x E[X] = lambda x E[X]. Var[S] = E[N] x E[X^2] = lambda x E[X^2].

Example: lambda = 100 claims, E[X] = 20000, E[X^2] = 600M. E[S] = 100 x 20000 = 2000000. Var[S] = 100 x 600M = 60,000M. SD = sqrt(60,000M) = 244949.

Topic 4: Premium Principles and Risk Loading

Estimated time: ~35 minutes | Objectives: Explain premium principles, calculate premium with loading, explain why loading is needed.

Premium must cover expected claims + expenses + profit + safety margin. If premium is too low, insurer goes broke. If too high, no one buys.

Expected value principle: P = (1 + theta) x E[S], theta = loading.

Variance principle: P = E[S] + alpha x Var[S].

Example: E[S] = 2000000, theta = 20%. P = 1.2 x 2000000 = 2400000. Loading = 400000 for expenses, profit, safety.

Topic 5: Reinsurance

Estimated time: ~35 minutes | Objectives: Explain reinsurance, distinguish proportional vs non-proportional, calculate split.

Reinsurance is insurance for insurers. The insurer keeps part of risk (retention) and passes the rest to reinsurer.

Proportional - Quota share: Insurer keeps a%, reinsurer takes (1-a)%. Example: 30% quota share, claim 1000000. Insurer pays 300000, reinsurer pays 700000.

Non-proportional - Excess of Loss (XL): Insurer pays up to M, reinsurer pays above M. Example: XL 500000 excess 500000. Claim 1200000. Insurer pays 500000, reinsurer pays 700000.

Topic 6: Ruin Theory and Credibility

Estimated time: ~35 minutes | Objectives: Explain ruin, explain surplus process, explain credibility, calculate credibility premium.

Ruin = insurer runs out of money and cannot pay claims. Credibility = how much to trust your own data vs industry data.

Surplus: U(t) = u + c t - S(t), where u = initial surplus, c = premium rate, S(t) = aggregate claims to time t. Ruin if U(t) < 0.

Credibility premium: P = Z x Own Experience + (1-Z) x Industry / Prior, Z = n / (n + K). More data (n) = higher Z = trust own data more.

Example: n = 100 claims, K = 50. Z = 100 / 150 = 0.667. Own premium 10000, industry 7000. Credibility premium = 0.667 x 10000 + 0.333 x 7000 = 6670 + 2331 = 9001.
