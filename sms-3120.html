Formula Sheet - SMS 3120

Premium: Premium = Expected Claims + Expenses + Profit | Indemnity: Pay the loss, not more | Contribution: Each insurer pays (Own Sum / Total Sum) x Loss | Excess: Insurer pays Loss - Excess | Loss Ratio: Claims / Premiums x 100%

Glossary - SMS 3120

General Insurance: non-life insurance - motor, fire, health, liability. Insured: person covered. Insurer: company that pays. Premium: price. Claim: request for payment. Excess / Deductible: first part you pay yourself. Underwriting: deciding to accept risk and at what price. Reinsurance: insurance for insurers. Indemnity: put back, not richer. Policy: contract.

Topic 1: Introduction to General Insurance

Estimated time: ~30 minutes | Objectives: Define general insurance, distinguish life vs general, explain why it exists, give examples.

General insurance covers things that are not life - your car, house, health, business, liability. Life insurance pays on death / survival. General insurance pays when a loss happens.

Why it exists: One fire can wipe out a business. One accident can cost millions. No one person can carry that alone, so many share the risk.

Examples: Motor, Fire, Health, Theft, Liability, Marine, Agriculture.

Short-term: Most general policies last 1 year and are renewed. Life policies last many years.

Topic 2: Principles of Insurance

Estimated time: ~35 minutes | Objectives: Explain 6 principles, apply each to a case, explain why they matter.

These 6 principles stop cheating and keep insurance fair. MUST loves asking you to apply them, not just list them.

  1. Insurable interest - you must lose if it is lost.
  2. Utmost good faith - tell truth, hide nothing important.
  3. Indemnity - put back, not richer.
  4. Subrogation - insurer can recover from causer.
  5. Contribution - if 2 insurers cover same loss, they share.
  6. Proximate cause - main cause must be covered.

Example - Indemnity: Car worth 500,000 is destroyed. Insurer pays 500,000, not 800,000, even if sum insured is 800,000.

Topic 3: Types of General Insurance

Estimated time: ~35 minutes | Objectives: Explain motor, fire, health, liability, theft, marine, give cover and exclusions.

Each type covers a different risk. In exams, always say what it covers + one example + one exclusion.

Motor: accident, theft, third-party injury. Exclusion: drunk driving.

Fire: fire, lightning, explosion damage to property. Exclusion: war.

Health: hospital, surgery, medicine. Exclusion: cosmetic surgery.

Liability: you pay someone else you injured / damaged. Example: customer slips in shop.

Theft / Burglary: stolen property after break-in.

Topic 4: Underwriting and Premiums

Estimated time: ~35 minutes | Objectives: Explain underwriting, explain factors that affect premium, calculate premium, explain excess.

Underwriting is deciding: Do we accept this risk? At what price? With what conditions? A bad risk is not always rejected - it is charged more.

Premium factors: Age, value, location, past claims, use, security, excess chosen.

Premium = Expected Claims + Expenses + Profit. Example: Expected claims 8000, expenses 2000, profit 1000. Premium = 11000.

Excess: First part you pay. Higher excess = lower premium, because you share risk.

Topic 5: Claims Handling

Estimated time: ~30 minutes | Objectives: Explain claims process, explain documents, explain fraud, explain settlement.

Claims is where insurance is tested. A good claims process is fast, fair, and checks fraud.

Steps: 1. Notify insurer quickly. 2. Fill claim form + documents (police report, photos, receipts). 3. Assessor / adjuster checks loss. 4. Insurer decides pay / negotiate / reject with reason. 5. Pay, less excess.

Fraud: Fake claim, inflated claim, staged accident. Fraud raises premiums for everyone.

Settlement: Cash, Repair, Replace, Reinstate - depending on policy.

Topic 6: Reinsurance and Regulation

Estimated time: ~30 minutes | Objectives: Explain reinsurance, explain why insurers need it, explain regulation, explain IRA in Kenya.

Even insurers need insurance. That is reinsurance. An insurer passes part of a big risk to a reinsurer, so one huge fire does not kill the insurer.

Reinsurance: Insurer keeps small part, reinsurer takes large part. Example: Insurer keeps 1,000,000, reinsurer pays above that.

Regulation: In Kenya, the Insurance Regulatory Authority (IRA) licenses insurers, protects policyholders, checks that insurers have enough money to pay claims.

Why regulate: People pay today for a promise tomorrow. Regulator makes sure the promise can be kept.
