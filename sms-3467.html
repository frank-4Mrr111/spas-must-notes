Formula Sheet - SMS 3467

Premium: Premium = Expected Loss + Expenses + Profit + Margin | Earned premium: Written - Change in UPR | UPR: Premium for cover not yet given | Aggregate loss: E(S) = E(N) x E(X) | Var(S) = E(N) Var(X) + Var(N) E(X)^2 | Chain ladder: Ultimate = Latest cumulative x Development factors | Reserve = Ultimate - Paid to date | Quota share: Ceded = c x Premium, Retained = (1 - c) x Premium | Surplus share: similar, on sum insured | Excess of loss: Reinsurer pays max(0, min(Claim - Deductible, Limit)) | Loss ratio: Incurred claims / Earned premium | Combined ratio: (Claims + Expenses) / Earned premium | IBNR: Incurred But Not Reported

Glossary - SMS 3467

Non-life / general insurance: motor, fire, medical, liability - not life. Premium: price of cover. Written premium: premium for policies sold. Earned premium: premium for cover already given. UPR: Unearned Premium Reserve - premium for cover still to give. Claim: money insurer pays. IBNR: claims happened but not yet reported. Case reserve: reserve for reported but not yet paid claims. Reserving: estimating future claim payments. Chain ladder: reserving by development factors. Reinsurance: insurance for insurers. Cedant: insurer passing risk. Reinsurer: company taking risk. Quota share: share every risk by fixed %. Excess of loss: reinsurer pays above deductible up to limit. Deductible / excess: amount policyholder or cedant pays first. Loss ratio: claims / earned premium. Combined ratio: claims + expenses / earned premium. Over 100% = underwriting loss.

Topic 1: Introduction to Non-Life Insurance - Premium, UPR, Claims

Estimated time: ~30 minutes | Objectives: Define non-life insurance, explain premium, earned premium, UPR, claims.

Non-life = short-term cover, usually 1 year. Motor, fire, medical, travel, liability. If no claim, cover ends - no savings back.

Premium parts: Expected loss + Expenses + Profit + Risk margin.

Written vs Earned: Written = sold. Earned = cover already given. UPR = cover still to give.

Example: Annual premium 120000, policy starts 1 October. At 31 December, 3 months earned. Earned = 120000 x 3/12 = 30000. UPR = 120000 - 30000 = 90000.

Claims: Reported, IBNR (happened but not reported), Case reserve (reported but not paid).

Topic 2: Claim Frequency, Severity and Aggregate Loss

Estimated time: ~40 minutes | Objectives: Explain frequency, severity, aggregate loss, calculate E(S) and Var(S).

Same as loss models, but now for pricing non-life.

Frequency N: Number of claims. Often Poisson. Severity X: Size of each claim.

Aggregate S: E(S) = E(N) x E(X). Var(S) = E(N) Var(X) + Var(N) E(X)^2.

Example: E(N)=100, E(X)=20000, Var(N)=100, Var(X)=900000000 (SD 30000). E(S)=100x20000=2000000. Var(S)=100x900000000 + 100x20000^2 = 90000000000 + 40000000000 = 130000000000. SD = 360555.

Loss ratio: Claims / Earned premium. Combined ratio = (Claims + Expenses) / Earned premium. Over 100% = underwriting loss.

Topic 3: Reserving - Chain Ladder, Bornhuetter-Ferguson

Estimated time: ~40 minutes | Objectives: Explain reserving, calculate chain ladder ultimate and reserve, explain Bornhuetter-Ferguson.

Reserving = how much will we still pay for claims already happened? Get it wrong = company looks rich, then fails.

Chain ladder: Ultimate = Latest paid x Development factors. Reserve = Ultimate - Paid to date.

Example: Paid to date 800000, cumulative development factor 1.25. Ultimate = 800000 x 1.25 = 1000000. Reserve = 1000000 - 800000 = 200000.

Bornhuetter-Ferguson (BF): Blend chain ladder with expected loss. Reserve = Expected ultimate x (1 - 1 / CDF). Good when data is thin or volatile.

IBNR: Ultimate - Reported to date. Case reserve is part of reported.

Topic 4: Reinsurance - Proportional, Excess of Loss

Estimated time: ~35 minutes | Objectives: Explain reinsurance, calculate quota share, calculate excess of loss split.

Reinsurance = insurance for insurers. Insurer passes part of risk, pays reinsurance premium.

Quota share: Share every risk by fixed %. Example: 40% quota share. Premium 1000000. Ceded = 400000. Retained = 600000. Claim 500000. Reinsurer pays 200000. Insurer keeps 300000.

Surplus share: Share only the part above retention, by sum insured.

Excess of loss (XL): Reinsurer pays above deductible, up to limit. Example: Deductible 1000000, Limit 3000000. Claim 5000000. Insurer pays first 1000000 + above limit 1000000 = 2000000. Reinsurer pays 3000000.

Why: 1. Protect against big claims. 2. Smooth results. 3. Increase capacity. 4. Meet regulator.

Topic 5: Premium Rating - GLM, Bonus-Malus, Credibility

Estimated time: ~40 minutes | Objectives: Explain rating factors, GLM, bonus-malus, credibility in rating.

Rating = charging different prices for different risks. A young driver in Nairobi is not the same risk as an old driver in Meru.

Rating factors: Age, sex, area, vehicle, use, claims history, sum insured.

GLM: Generalised Linear Model - fit claim frequency and severity to rating factors. Multiplicative tariff: Base premium x Age factor x Area factor x ...

Example: Base 20000, Age factor 1.5, Area factor 1.2. Premium = 20000 x 1.5 x 1.2 = 36000.

Bonus-malus: No-claim discount. Claim = move up malus, pay more. No claim = move down bonus, pay less.

Credibility: Blend own experience with group, as in SMS 3465.

Topic 6: Non-Life in Practice - Solvency, Ruin, Kenya

Estimated time: ~30 minutes | Objectives: Explain solvency and ruin, explain Kenya non-life practice.

Non-life can fail fast - one flood, one fire, many claims at once.

Solvency: Assets must cover liabilities + capital. Kenya: 【entity-IRA¦canonical_name=IRA】 sets rules, Risk-Based Capital.

Ruin: Surplus falls to zero. More capital, more reinsurance, better pricing = lower ruin chance.

Kenya non-life: Motor is biggest class. Medical growing. Fraud, uninsured drivers, price wars, delays in claims. 【entity-IRA¦canonical_name=IRA】, AKI (Association of Kenya Insurers), NTSA for motor.

Underwriting cycle: Soft market - low prices, losses. Hard market - high prices, profit. Do not follow the crowd down.
