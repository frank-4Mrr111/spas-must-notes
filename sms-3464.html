Glossary - SMS 3464

Risk: chance of loss. Market risk: loss from prices moving. Credit risk: borrower does not pay. Liquidity risk: cannot sell / pay when needed. Operational risk: loss from people, systems, fraud. VaR: worst loss at given confidence. Expected Shortfall: average loss beyond VaR. Hedging: reducing risk with opposite position. Derivative: contract whose value comes from another asset. Forward: agree today to buy / sell later. Option: right, not duty, to buy / sell. Swap: exchange cashflows. Capital: buffer to absorb losses. Basel: bank capital rules. Topic 1: Introduction to Financial Risk - Types, Measurement, Risk Appetite

Estimated time: ~30 minutes | Objectives: Define risk, list risk types, explain risk measurement, explain risk appetite.

Risk = chance of loss. Risk management = identify, measure, reduce, monitor. You cannot remove all risk - you choose which risk to take.

Types: Market (prices move), Credit (borrower fails), Liquidity (cannot sell / pay), Operational (fraud, system, people), Insurance (claims bigger than expected).

Measure: How big is loss? How often? Use: Variance, VaR, Expected Shortfall, Stress test.

Risk appetite: How much risk the company is willing to take. Risk limit: maximum allowed. Breach = stop, report, fix.

Example: An insurer accepts insurance risk, but hedges market risk - it does not want to lose money because share prices fell.

Topic 2: Market Risk - VaR, Expected Shortfall, Backtesting

Estimated time: ~40 minutes | Objectives: Explain market risk, calculate VaR and Expected Shortfall, explain backtesting.

Market risk = loss because prices move: shares, interest rates, exchange rates.

VaR: Worst loss at given confidence over given time. 95% 1-day VaR = 1000000 means: 95% chance loss is less than 1000000 tomorrow. 5% chance it is worse.

Normal VaR: VaR = (z x sigma - mu) x Value. 95%: z=1.65. 99%: z=2.33. Example: Value 100000000, sigma 2%, mu 0. 95% VaR = 1.65 x 2% x 100000000 = 3300000.

Expected Shortfall (ES): Average loss when loss is worse than VaR. ES is better - it tells how bad bad days are. VaR does not.

Backtesting: Count how many times actual loss beat VaR. If VaR is 95%, expect about 5% breaches. Many more = model is wrong.

Topic 3: Credit Risk - Default, Exposure, Credit VaR

Estimated time: ~35 minutes | Objectives: Explain credit risk, calculate expected loss, explain PD, LGD, EAD.

Credit risk = borrower does not pay. For an insurer: reinsurer fails, bond issuer fails, premium debtor fails.

Three parts: PD = Probability of Default. LGD = Loss Given Default (share lost if default). EAD = Exposure At Default (how much is owed).

Expected Loss: EL = PD x LGD x EAD. Example: PD 2%, LGD 50%, EAD 10000000. EL = 0.02 x 0.5 x 10000000 = 100000.

Unexpected loss: Actual loss can be bigger than EL. Hold capital for unexpected loss.

Reduce: Diversify (many borrowers), collateral, credit limits, rating (AAA to D).

Topic 4: Liquidity Risk and Operational Risk

Estimated time: ~35 minutes | Objectives: Explain liquidity risk, explain operational risk, give controls.

Liquidity risk = cannot pay when due. You can be rich on paper and still fail - because your money is locked in buildings, not cash.

Liquidity: Funding liquidity - cannot raise cash. Market liquidity - cannot sell asset without big loss. Example: Insurer must pay claims after flood, but its money is in property that takes months to sell.

Manage: Hold cash / liquid assets, match assets to liabilities (ALM), stress test - what if many claims come at once?

Operational risk: Loss from people, systems, processes, fraud, external events. Example: Staff fraud, system crash, wrong data, cyber attack.

Manage: Controls, segregation of duties, audit, backup, insurance for operational risk.

Topic 5: Hedging and Derivatives - Forwards, Options, Swaps

Estimated time: ~40 minutes | Objectives: Explain hedging, explain forwards, options, swaps, calculate hedge and forward price.

Hedging = take opposite position to reduce risk. You give up some gain to avoid big loss.

Forward: Agree today to buy / sell at fixed price later. Example: Importer will pay USD 100000 in 3 months. Forward locks KES/USD rate today, so a weaker shilling does not hurt.

Option: Right, not duty, to buy (call) or sell (put) at strike K. You pay premium. If price moves against you, use option. If not, let it expire.

Swap: Exchange cashflows. Interest rate swap: exchange fixed for floating. Currency swap: exchange KES for USD cashflows.

Forward price: F = S e^(rT). Example: S=100, r=10%, T=1 year. F = 100 x e^0.10 = 110.52.

Topic 6: Risk Management in Practice - Capital, Regulation, Kenya

Estimated time: ~30 minutes | Objectives: Explain capital, explain Basel and Solvency, explain Kenya regulation.

Capital = buffer. When losses come, capital absorbs them, so the company does not fail and policyholders still get paid.

Why hold capital: 1. Absorb unexpected losses. 2. Pay claims in bad times. 3. Meet regulator. 4. Keep rating and trust.

Basel (banks): Banks must hold capital based on risk - credit, market, operational. Higher risk = more capital.

Solvency (insurers): Insurers must hold capital based on insurance, market, credit, operational risk. Kenya: IRA sets solvency / capital rules. Risk-Based Capital (RBC).

Kenya: CBK regulates banks, IRA regulates insurers, CMA regulates capital markets. All require risk management, reporting, stress tests.
